# Future Ideas

Candidates for the simulation, not planned work. Worthwhile ones get formalized as ARDs when their time comes.

If you discover a new mechanism during implementation that could meaningfully affect collapse/thrive dynamics, add it here with a one-sentence note on why it matters. Don't implement without an ARD.

Items are grouped by priority — a working judgment, not a commitment. Promote, demote, or split as the model evolves. Within each tier, original category labels (Mechanics / Behavioral feedback / Social structure / Research / Output) are preserved.

## Very useful

Sharpens the collapse/thrive signal or experimental setup, but does not cause degenerate outcomes on its own.

### Mechanics

**Frontier expansion — a resource ceiling that isn't structurally fixed**
Every non-extinct configuration found so far (`docs/research-scale-robustness.md`) works within a
fixed `MAX_NATURAL_RESOURCE_CEILING`: invention's ceiling-growth branch only pushes the *current*
ceiling toward that constant (ARD 047, deliberate hard cap), never moves the cap itself. That forecloses
testing a specific hypothesis raised in session: that sustained innovation *combined with* discovering
new resource pools — not just extracting the existing one more efficiently — could produce genuine
open-ended growth rather than the boom-bust cycling every tested config produces. Two single-seed
traces this session (biased 2:1 `INVENTION_DEPLETION_FASTER_WEIGHT`) showed why the distinction
matters: pushing extraction efficiency up against a *fixed* pool just drains it faster and
permanently (pool at 0% from tick 299 onward, versus the frozen-productivity baseline's cycling
between 0% and 80–99%) — efficiency without new supply is not the same lever as new supply.
A candidate mechanism: some event (plausibly invention, or pressure from a near-exhausted pool)
has a chance to unlock a new resource pool with its own ceiling and regen budget, at a cost or
probability that gets harder each time — not a literal unbounded ceiling, which would trivially
solve the model and stop being worth studying, but a frontier that has to keep being re-earned. This
is a new mechanic with several non-obvious choices (trigger condition, cost curve, how a second pool
interacts with the existing one-pool Gini/consumption/welfare machinery) and needs an ARD before any
code — not implemented here.
*(Tension worth resolving first, 2026-09-18: `docs/research-productivity-band.md` finds that in this model innovation variance drives extinction risk hard in the one regime tested (300 founders, scaled commons, 3000 ticks): freezing extraction productivity or holding it within ±2× gives 0 of 24 extinct, while ±10× gives 17 of 24. Not checked outside that regime, and not an audit of every configuration in the project's history. This entry supposes sustained innovation is a route to abundance. Both cannot be straightforwardly true, and which one holds depends on whether new supply changes the sign of the upward tail, since today a run that innovates its way to high productivity strips the fixed pool and dies. Designing the frontier mechanism without settling that is likely to produce another delaying config.)*

**`extractionProductivity`'s bounds are log-asymmetric — the drift ARD 047 removed came back** — see `docs/research-thriving-reachability.md`
ARD 047 made the faster/slower invention branches exact multiplicative inverses so "a faster–slower pair returns productivity to its prior value exactly," leaving the long-run behaviour to "the floor, the cap, and the weights — all explicit dials." The step is unbiased, but the band is not: `[EXTRACTION_PRODUCTIVITY_FLOOR=0.01, MAX_EXTRACTION_PRODUCTIVITY=10]` gives 4.6 log-units of room below the starting 1.0 and 2.3 above, so the reflected walk is asymptotically uniform over that band — Monte Carlo (200 chains × 4000 steps) gives median 0.35×, `P(<1)=0.65`, `P(<0.2)=0.42`. Gather output is linear in productivity, so the long-run economy is ~3× poorer than calibrated, and a run can starve with the commons at 100% of ceiling (directly observed at prod 0.03–0.10). Unpinning productivity takes the thriving-existence config from THRIVING×2 to EXTINCTION×2; a log-symmetric band (`FLOOR = 1/MAX`) recovers most of it. Same class of defect as the age-38 fertility window (ARD 059): an unchosen number sitting under a pessimistic result. ARD-level — it changes how every long-horizon result since ARD 047 should be read.

> **Re-measured 2026-09-18 (commit 2d13d25) — the diagnosis above is wrong in its conclusion, and the proposed fix does not work.** See `docs/research-productivity-band.md`. What predicts survival is the band's *width*, not its asymmetry. At 24 seeds / 3000 ticks / 300 founders with the scaled commons, a log-symmetric `[0.1, 10]` still loses **17 of 24 seeds**, while a log-symmetric `[0.5, 2]` loses **none** (paired test, extinction predicted in advance, ~1 in 65,536). The Monte Carlo above is right that a wide asymmetric band drifts down, but it only measured the downward tail: a run that wanders *high* strips the commons faster than it regenerates, and a symmetric band keeps both tails. The `FLOOR = 1/MAX` fix would therefore have been measured, found wanting, and blamed on something else. Do not treat this as "the cheapest high-leverage fix" — narrowing the band far enough to survive makes `InventionEvent` nearly decorative, which is the pin in another costume. The open question is a bounded-variance process that keeps invention meaningful (mean reversion toward 1.0, or a step size shrinking away from centre); that is a new mechanism, not a constant change.

**Per-capita levers only work once the commons stops binding — sequence the calibration work** — see `docs/research-untested-variables.md`
At default settings, raising `BASE_CHILDBIRTH_RATE` from 0.6 to 1.0 moves peak population by −0.8% and leaves 48/48 extinct; the same change under the productivity pin takes extinction to 1/48 at 2000 ticks and 6/48 at 8000 (p=7e-15), cutting the hazard from ~24% to ~2% per 1000 ticks. `HAPPINESS_BASELINE` and `EXPERIENCE_CAP` show the same flat-then-decisive shape. So "no single constant fixes overshoot→extinction" is partly an artifact of every constant having been tested in the one regime where per-capita constants cannot matter — the pool caps the population regardless of how many are born or how much each extracts. Implication for planning, not a mechanism to build: commons-side fixes (the soft brake and the productivity band above) should land *before* further per-capita calibration, because that work is unmeasurable until they do; then re-run the archived nulls. Note the ceiling on this: even the 12× config's hazard stays constant out to 16,000 ticks, so it delays rather than escapes, and `BASE_CHILDBIRTH_RATE=1.0` exceeds natural fertility anyway.

**Subsidised children are scored as destitute by the happiness getter** — see `docs/research-untested-variables.md`
Children with living parents pay `resources × CONSUMPTION_CHILD_RESOURCE_RATE` rather than a flat cost (ARD 024), so they cannot starve, and ARD 062 makes welfare skip them for exactly that reason. But `Person.happiness` scores them on their own balance, and they hold a mean of 3.2 resources with **91% below the critical threshold**, so they take the full −5 poverty penalty: a child in a prosperous household reads as maximally poor. Two subsystems treat a subsidised child as not in need and the third treats them as maximally in need. At 24–31% of the population this costs ~1.1–1.5 points of mean happiness, against a THRIVING gate of 6.0 the model currently misses at 2.96 — and happiness is the gate that fails in **16/16 runs of every configuration tested**, so this is directly load-bearing on whether THRIVING is reachable at all. Fix options: score a subsidised child on household resources (parents' mean), exempt them from the resource term entirely, or give them their own threshold. ARD-level — it changes an agent-level computed property and every happiness-derived result in the project.

**THRIVING's happiness condition is a second commons gate, not an independent dimension** — see `docs/research-untested-variables.md`
ARD 051 treats the four THRIVING conditions as separate dimensions, but the happiness one is dominated by its resource term: the +3 comfortable bonus needs 70 resources against an adult mean of 16.6, so it almost never fires while the low/critical penalties fire constantly, leaving the resource term negative for every cohort (−4.72 children, −3.00 adults, −2.74 elders) and every other component near its maximum (employment is 94%). Happiness therefore only clears 6.0 in a resource-abundant society — which is what the commons condition already asks. Lowering the comfort thresholds does not decouple them (mean rises 2.96 → 4.34, gate still fails 16/16). Worth deciding deliberately whether THRIVING should require resource abundance twice, or whether the happiness condition should be rebuilt on the non-resource components.

**THRIVING's happiness gate is an absolute threshold on an uncalibrated scale** — see `docs/research-untested-variables.md`
`HAPPINESS_BASELINE = 0` has no empirical anchor (its own comment says "raise if too many persons floor at 0"), happiness is floored at zero with no ceiling, and ARD 051's `THRIVING_HAPPINESS_THRESHOLD = 6.0` is an absolute threshold on the mean of that quantity — the two were set independently with no shared referent. Raising the baseline to 5 takes the happiness gate from failing in 8/8 seeds to passing in every surviving seed, and `ANY-DECADE-THRIVE` from 0/8 to 8/8, while 48-seed extinction stays 48/48: the constant moves the label and not one death. Same class as ARD 059's age-38 fertility window, though weaker — a happiness scale has no natural zero to be *wrong* about, which is exactly why an absolute threshold on it cannot mean anything. Fix the gate (a quantile, or a fraction of the maximum attainable from the bonus constants), not the baseline; raising the baseline would manufacture THRIVING labels. ARD-level — it changes an ARD-051 outcome dimension.

**Record realised per-person extraction, not just potential** — see `docs/calibration-guide.md`
`scripts/flow-probe.ts` can only report *potential* extraction, because `GatherResourcesEvent` takes `min(output, naturalResources)` and discards what each person actually got. That inverts the probe's headline whenever the pool binds: at default extraction the share of people short of their own living cost reads 0–6% while the pool sits at 0% — almost nobody short on paper, almost everybody short in fact. `Simulation` already has the pattern (`recordConsumption`, `tickConsumption`), so the shape is a matching `recordExtraction` with a per-tick array cleared each tick, sampled by the decade hook — memory bounded by population, not by run length. ARD-level: it adds a recorded per-person stat, and the choice of what to retain (per-tick array vs quantiles vs a running sketch) is exactly the kind of thing a future reader would have to guess at.

**Resource thresholds are asserted in absolute units, not derived from what a person costs** — see `docs/research-extraction-need-ratio.md`
One tick is one year and an adult consumes `CONSUMPTION_BASE = 1.0` per year (1.5 past 65). Every *stock* threshold in the model is a bare number in the same unit, so each one silently asserts a number of years of living costs: fertility floor 10, welfare line 20, fertility-full and happiness-"low" 30, happiness-"comfortable" 70, elderly-comfortable 100 (~67 years at 1.5/yr), seeded adult mean 50, and one birth costing each parent 12. Nothing derived any of them from the consumption rate, and they do not agree with each other. Two consequences. **They re-anchor whenever extraction changes**: equilibrium holdings are `(extraction − consumption) / TAX_RATE`, so the default economy settles at ~144 — clear of every threshold — while the cut-extraction config settles at ~27, wedged between the welfare line (20) and happiness-"low" (30). That is why a pure extraction change moved happiness onto its STRUGGLING gate and made `welf%` incomparable across arms. **And welfare then manufactures the answer**: measured with `scripts/flow-probe.ts`, median holdings sit at 15.7–22.1 across an entire run of the cut config and oscillate around 20 in the default, because `distributeWelfare` tops every short person up to exactly `WELFARE_THRESHOLD`. So the model's revealed answer to "how much does a person need" is 20 — an asserted constant, not an economic outcome — and every subsystem keying on personal resources is reading a number the welfare system sets. Fix is to express the stock thresholds as multiples of annual consumption. ARD-level: it re-parameterises five subsystems at once, and picking the multiples is exactly the judgment a future reader would otherwise have to guess at. Strong instance of the constants-audit entry below.

**Newborn intents regress toward zero, so every behavioural disposition dies out in three generations** — found 2026-09-19
`ChildbirthEvent.drawStat` regresses a newborn's stat toward `NEWBORN_STAT_POPULATION_MEAN` (5.5): `mean + (parentMean − mean) × coeff + noise`. `drawIntent` has no anchor at all: `parentMean × coeff + noise`, which regresses toward **zero**. Simulated over the actual draw, mean `learningIntent` goes 0.50 (seeded) → 0.12 → 0.034 → 0.017 and settles near **0.014** — a 35× collapse in three generations, where stats hold their mean indefinitely. Enrollment probability is `BASE_ENROLLMENT_RATE × learningIntent × ageModifier`, so it falls from ~0.025/tick to ~0.0007/tick: about a 3% chance of starting *one* education level in a whole life. Directly visible in the HTML report — founders carry the seeded 85% high-school rate, and the education chart is ~100% "None" for the remaining 7,900 years. The same draw governs `exerciseIntent`, `stealingIntent` and `killingIntent`, so after ~3 generations nobody learns, exercises, steals or kills by disposition and only the unconditional events still run. **This is the most likely explanation for the project's long history of per-capita levers reading as inert**: many of them gate on an intent that had already collapsed to ~0.014 by the time any measurement was taken. Deciding whether intents should anchor to a population mean (like stats), to the seeded distribution, or genuinely to zero is ARD-level — it changes what heritability means in this model and will move every long-horizon result.

**Newborns never inherit `helpingIntent` at all** — found 2026-09-19
`Person.helpingIntent` defaults to 0, `Simulation.seed` draws founders from [0, 0.5), and `ChildbirthEvent.seedNewborn` sets `learningIntent`, `exerciseIntent`, `stealingIntent` and `killingIntent` — but not `helpingIntent`. So every person born inside a run has `helpingIntent` exactly 0 and `HelpEvent` can never fire for them; only the founding generation ever helps anyone. ARD 045 added the field and seeded it deliberately higher than the antisocial intents, and childbirth was never updated to carry it. Distinct from the entry above: that one is a design question about the regression target, this is a field simply omitted from a list. A plain fix (add the line), but it is not behaviour-neutral, so it wants its own measurement pass rather than a quiet patch.

**The resource ceiling saturates in the first 50 ticks and never moves again** — found 2026-09-19
Measured on the best-known config: `naturalResourceCeiling` reaches `MAX_NATURAL_RESOURCE_CEILING` by tick ~50 and sits there for **98% of a 2,000-tick run**. Because regeneration is `ceiling × NATURAL_RESOURCE_REGEN_FRACTION`, inflow is therefore pinned at a constant for essentially the whole run, and `MAX_NATURAL_RESOURCE_CEILING × NATURAL_RESOURCE_REGEN_FRACTION ÷ CONSUMPTION_BASE` is a **hard cap on sustainable population** that no amount of technology, fertility or efficiency can exceed: ~700 at defaults, ~2,100 in the scaled-commons config, ~7,100 at the 1,000-founder scale. Measured peak population under cut extraction is 2,185 and 6,502 against those caps — i.e. sitting on them. Two consequences worth separating: `InventionEvent`'s ceiling-growth branch, weighted highest of the three at 2:1:1, is inert after the first 50 years; and any claim that a configuration "stabilises" the population has to rule out that the population is simply pinned against this constant. Also note regen keys off the ceiling rather than the current stock, so a pool stripped to zero still regenerates at full rate — the opposite of a logistic renewable resource, and why the commons always recovers during a population trough.

**Welfare clamps the fertility brake's input, so density-dependent fertility cannot work as built** — see `docs/research-extraction-need-ratio.md`
`ChildbirthEvent` scales births from zero at `CHILDBIRTH_RESOURCE_MIN` (10) to full at `CHILDBIRTH_RESOURCE_SCALE` (30), reading the *parents' personal resources*. But `distributeWelfare` tops every short person up to `WELFARE_THRESHOLD` (20), so instrumenting couple resources through a full boom-bust cycle finds the median couple sitting at ~20 in both boom and recovery. The brake's input is pinned by the welfare system rather than tracking the commons, so it reads a near-constant value however depleted the pool is. Moving the brake later turns it into a kill switch, not a brake: `CHILDBIRTH_RESOURCE_SCALE` at 90/150/300 gives 8/8 extinct at every step, because the clamped couple's fertility factor collapses toward zero while their resources never move. Any density-dependent fertility mechanism has to resolve this first — either the brake reads something welfare does not set (commons fill, local density), or the two constants are calibrated as a pair. ARD-level: it changes what a fertility gate is allowed to key on.

**Production has no dependency ratio: gathering is age-blind while consumption is not** — see `docs/research-untested-variables.md`
*(Cleanup half done 2026-09-17: the orphaned `GATHERING_PEAK_AGE`/`GATHERING_AGE_SCALE`/`GATHERING_AGE_FLOOR` trio is deleted, and a comment at the age-profile block records that `GatherResourcesEvent` is deliberately age-blind per ODD §7. The mechanism half below is untouched and still needs an ARD.)*
The substantive half: consumption is age-scaled (`CONSUMPTION_ELDER_MULTIPLIER`) while production is not, so a 90-year-old extracts like a 30-year-old of equal experience, experience is lifetime-cumulative, and `ELDERLY_IDLENESS_DECAY` is a measured null — the model has no production-side dependency ratio at all. That matters because the default collapse is visibly an age-structure event (median age climbs 18 → 51 → 91 as births stop). Wiring an age profile into gather is ARD-level — it is a new mechanism, and Part 1 predicts it will read as inert until the commons is fixed.

**Peak-decline is measured against an all-time maximum, so one overshoot disqualifies every later steady state** — see `docs/research-untested-variables.md`
`Reporters.ts:156` takes `peakPop` over the whole run history and THRIVING needs the final population within 15% of it, so the reference point only ratchets up. A population that overshoots once and then settles into a genuinely sustainable steady state at 60% of its historical high reads as COLLAPSE forever. Combined with the soft-brake item below (the model *must* overshoot, since gather ignores pool fill), this puts the peak-decline and commons conditions in direct opposition: being at your population peak means the most mouths on the pool, which is exactly when it is stripped. Measured per run across 48 runs: the two move in opposite directions in **45/48 runs at default and 48/48** for the strongest config, and the split is close to absolute — both conditions hold together on ~82% of ticks **before** the commons is first emptied and on **0.0–0.2% after**. So THRIVING's structural half is the normal state during the approach and becomes unreachable the moment the pool is first stripped. Two separable fixes: a soft brake (model), or measuring decline against a trailing window rather than an all-time max (measurement). Worth deciding independently. ARD-level either way — it changes an ARD-051 outcome dimension or the commons dynamics.

**The commons has no feedback before exhaustion (soft brake)** — see `docs/research-thriving-reachability.md`
`GatherResourcesEvent` extracts `min(output, naturalResources)`; output does not scale with pool fill. There is no negative feedback in the interior, so the pool is bang-bang: if `N × gather < regen` it fills to the ceiling, otherwise it drains to zero, and the population's only signal is starvation *after* exhaustion. That is why every default run overshoots, and why ARD 051's 40%-commons gate is either trivially passed or catastrophically failed — the model cannot sit at a partly-drawn commons. Scaling gather output by fill fraction (or making fertility/consumption respond to it) turns overshoot into approach. Complements the crash-recovery item below rather than replacing it: anti-Allee rescues the trough, a soft brake prevents the crash. ARD-level (new feedback + calibration). *(Third line of evidence, 2026-09-19, `docs/research-extraction-need-ratio.md`: the overshoot ratio is now measured, not just argued. People extract ~3.9/tick against a consumption of 1.0 and a fixed regen flow of 900/tick, so the pool empties at ~230 extractors while consumption alone would support ~900 — a ~4x overshoot before any brake engages. Cutting extraction toward need shrinks the boom-bust from ~90-fold to ~8-fold and takes the commons from stripped 41% of ticks to 15%, which is the soft brake's predicted effect obtained by calibration instead of by mechanism. It does not stop extinction, so the mechanism is still wanted — but its target can now be stated numerically: hold extraction near 1.5x consumption without starving the low-ability tail, which a fill-scaled gather does automatically and a constant cut does not.)* *(Second line of evidence, 2026-09-17, `docs/research-escape-velocity.md`: the missing interior feedback is also what makes the model's future unpredictable from its present. Inside the productivity-pinned arm — the only arm producing both survivors and deaths — the runs that went extinct had reached **higher** values than the survivors on all nine measured quantities (population, commons fill, ecological surplus, equality, happiness, peak-proximity, employment, fertile share, productivity), on two independent 24-seed sets. The survivors are the runs too small to overshoot, sitting at a 3% median commons fill. With no signal before starvation, every interior state reads fine, so no threshold on any of them predicts survival. Re-running that probe after the brake lands is a cheap direct test of whether the brake did what it is meant to do.)*

**Universal vs targeted welfare — deserves a deliberate experiment** — see `docs/research-thriving-reachability.md`
ARD 034 rejected UBI on the grounds that it doesn't target the distress driving collapse, and ARD 061 leaves that standing. But the thriving-reachability study found the opposite signal: making welfare *universal* (threshold above everyone's resources, i.e. a flat dividend funded by a 10% tax) was one of the load-bearing changes in the only configuration that sustained THRIVING to 800 ticks, holding Gini at 0.02–0.16; dropping it alone took that config to STRUGGLING on Gini 0.53. Measured in a heavily modified config, so not evidence for reversing ARD 034 — but strong enough that universality should be tested against the ARD-061 top-up rather than assumed worse. Run after ARD 061 lands, alongside the redistribution-calibration item below (same channel, same sweep).

*(The welfare-concentration defect that prompted this — an equal split of the whole pot handing a handful of people the entire tax take, driving Gini 0.17 → 0.86 in a healthy population — is subsumed by ARD 061.)*

**Redistribution calibration vs the empirical ~25% Gini compression** — see `docs/research-taxation-welfare.md`
ARD 034's `TAX_RATE` (0.02), `WELFARE_THRESHOLD` (20), and `COMMUNITY_POOL_RESERVE_FRACTION` (0.20) have no empirical anchor, yet they are the most direct lever on the Gini coefficient (the primary collapse signal). OECD data gives a clean, in-sim-checkable target: taxes+transfers reduce market-income Gini by **~25% on average** (range ~5–40%), with *transfers* (bottom-weighted, already how ARD 034 targets welfare) doing most of the work. The current flat 2% channel looks near-inert — the community pool ends a run at ~18 against ~2,600 total resources — so it is unlikely to be compressing Gini meaningfully. **The welfare instrumentation (2026-09-14) makes this concrete: eligibility is near-universal while the payout is nil.** A 300-tick seed-7 run has 33–70% of the population below `WELFARE_THRESHOLD` every decade (peak 386 of 555 persons) against a pool of ~111 — roughly 1 resource each against a threshold of 20. Sweeps read `welf%` 42–62%. So the lever isn't "who qualifies" but "how much flows"; a means test that catches most of the population is a funding problem, not a targeting one. Recommendation: adopt "disposable Gini ≈ 0.7–0.85 × market Gini" as the calibration objective (measure via a redistribution-off baseline run), raise `TAX_RATE`/lower the reserve until the channel circulates, and add mild progressivity only if a flat rate can't reach the target. Tuning + a small ARD if progressivity is added.

**Replace `peakGini` as the sweep harness's inequality column, and `orphPk%` alongside it** — see `docs/research-gini-metric.md`
Per-tick instrumentation suggests `peakGini` is attained during the terminal crash at populations of ~10–30, making it a max-of-noise statistic that reads 0.79–0.89 for nearly every config — including configs whose actual inequality differs fourfold (`TAX_RATE` 0 vs 0.20 gives mature-phase Gini 0.574 vs 0.142 while `peakGini` reports 0.733 vs 0.714, and in one spot check ordered them backwards). It cuts both ways: re-testing found `CONSUMPTION_ELDER_MULTIPLIER`'s clean monotone `peakGini` trend (0.72→0.90) does *not* reproduce on a mature-phase median, so the column manufactures apparent effects as well as hiding real ones. If it holds, every calibration read off that column is suspect, and the fix is measurement, not parameters. Hypothesis only, and the same study puts the resolution limit at ~±0.05 `matureGini` at 10 seeds — which most single-lever effects ever measured here fall inside. Establish the noise floor and a defensible window definition first; changing the harness's headline metric is ARD-level since it redefines how every sweep is read. *(2026-09-18: `orphPk%` has the identical defect and the same fix applies — it is a worst-single-tick share whose denominator collapses at a cycle trough, so the best-known config reports 0.2% pooled against 100% peak. A pooled `orph%` column was added beside it as an immediate mitigation (`scripts/sweep.ts`, no ARD — purely additive, no definition changed), but deciding whether the peak column should exist at all belongs with this entry.)*

**Crash recovery / age-structure (would make collapse survivable)** — see `docs/research-fertility.md`, `docs/research-tuning-defaults.md`
The population is an intrinsic boom-bust oscillator (accepted as a HANDY-style behavior, ARD 050 + base-rate calibration), and crashes hit *zero* because the survivors of a boom are old and sparse — a thinned population can't rebuild (partnership-density Allee effect + synchronized initial cohort). This is the one lever fertility tuning can't supply. Options: seed a spread/younger initial age structure (and/or some children) to damp the founding cohort wave; or a low-density fertility/partnership boost so crashes bounce off a floor instead of going extinct. **Now the top structural priority:** the tuning-defaults study (`docs/research-tuning-defaults.md`) confirmed *no* parameter regime yields a sane long-run default — every single-lever sweep is a terminal one-shot overshoot (`cyc=0, stable=0`). Two throwaway probes showed which fix matters: pyramid seeding alone does **nothing** (the founding wave washes out and the synchronized cohort regenerates endogenously), but an **anti-Allee low-density fertility boost** (`p *= 1 + boost·max(0, 1−N/ref)`) moved `stable` off zero (2–3/16 seeds at strong settings). It is necessary but not sufficient on its own — a strong boost still lost ~70% of seeds because crash survivors are too old/unpartnered to rescue — so design it together with a second channel (weakened partnership-density dependence at low N, or younger continuously-replenished age structure). One existing lever is a complementary mitigator (not a substitute): a large `BASE_INVENTION_RATE` increase (~15×) yields `stable=6/16` at 800 ticks by continuously lifting/jostling `K` so the demographic wave rides a moving ceiling — but it too leaves the majority extinct, and only at an implausibly high invention rate. *(⚠ Re-measured 2026-09-14 at 48 seeds: `stable` is 6/48 (12.5%) vs a matched default control's 2/48 (4.2%), p=0.27 — the sustained-cycle effect is **not** established. The 800-tick extinction reduction (95.8% → 81.2%) does not survive either: at 2000 ticks invention is 48/48 extinct, exactly equal to default. It delays total extinction by ~800 ticks and prevents nothing. Also note the anti-Allee probe's own 2–3/16 figure above rests on the same n=16 basis and has never been re-measured, so the "moved `stable` off zero" claim for it is equally unverified. Design the crash-recovery ARD against trough depth, which is measurable per-run, rather than against `stable` counts that need 48+ seeds to resolve.)* *(Design note, 2026-09-14, `docs/research-untested-variables.md`: the case for making the boost **low-density-targeted** rather than uniform is now weaker than it looks. Under the productivity pin, a fertility rise concentrated on the distressed — `HAPPINESS_BASELINE=10`, which floors the happiness-fertility multiplier for exactly the people a trough contains — gave 7/48 extinct, while a *uniform* rise of matched average size (`BASE_CHILDBIRTH_RATE=0.83`) gave 2/48, and 1.0 gave 1/48. The difference is not significant (p=0.16) but runs opposite to what targeting predicts, so uniform fertility should be the control arm any anti-Allee design has to beat — not assumed inferior. Both still only delay: the 12× config's hazard is flat out to 16,000 ticks.)* ARD-level (new mechanic + non-obvious calibration); discuss with owner first. Sweep-validate per the protocol in `research-tuning-defaults.md` (target: `stable` rises and extinction share falls *without* suppressing the boom peak or erasing outcome variety).

**Orphanhood should cost something, and the flow should be measured**
The orphan series is now instrumented (per-tick `orphanCount`/`childPopulation`, reported and swept), but being orphaned carries no penalty beyond losing the ARD 024 parental consumption subsidy and the ARD 014 parental-resources basis for happiness — no mortality, education, or later-life effect. If crashes orphan children en masse, that is exactly the channel that turns a survivable dip into extinction, which makes it relevant to the crash-recovery work above. Two pieces: measure the *flow* (orphaning events per tick, not just the standing stock) so a crash's orphan wave is visible, and decide whether orphanhood needs a consequence (raised illness/mortality, blocked enrollment) or a mitigation (fostering by an unrelated couple, which the relationship system could carry). ARD-level if any consequence is added; the flow metric alone is measurement.

**Termination conditions**
Currently a fixed tick count. Worth adding: population=0 (already fires EXTINCTION but doesn't halt early), collapse-detected (Gini threshold + declining population over N ticks), or a flag to halt when outcome is determined rather than running to the end.

**Illness reduces gathering capacity**
Illness currently affects only mortality and happiness. Adding `potential *= (1 - person.illness)` makes illness a resource drain too, strengthening the collapse loop.

**Contagious illness / epidemic spread**
`MisfortuneEvent` rolls illness mortality independently per person. Real epidemics propagate by contact and have driven historical collapses. Add a contagion term proportional to the share of nearby ill. Requires a proximity model (preferred) or global mixing as a stopgap. Depends on `person.illness` being live (ARD 018).

**Resource pooling in relationships**
`isInRelationshipWith` is a flag with no economic consequence. Households pool income and consumption — pooling lowers measured inequality and changes household resilience. Decision: pool fully, partially, or treat the household as the economic unit.

**Voluntary trade / bilateral exchange (Sugarscape — Epstein & Axtell 1996; Tainter 1988; Cline, *1177 B.C.* 2014; Kander et al., *Entropy* 2024)**
Every resource transfer in the model is zero-sum or negative-sum (theft, tax, gift, windfall, inheritance); voluntary trade is the missing positive-sum channel where two agents each hold a surplus of something the other needs and both come out ahead. Trade network contraction is a documented leading indicator of collapse — the Late Bronze Age systems collapse is the canonical case, where interdependent Mycenaean, Hittite, and Egyptian exchange circuits failed in sequence within decades — while robust exchange buffers local extraction failures and dampens Gini. Sugarscape, the direct agent-ABM ancestor of this simulation's Gini focus, includes bilateral trade as a core mechanic; without it, the model has no mechanism to generate the surplus that distinguishes thriving civilizations, and can only redistribute scarcity, which may explain why THRIVING is rare in practice.

**Elite extraction differential / HANDY class hierarchy (Motesharrei, Rivas & Kalnay 2014; Turchin — *Secular Cycles* 2009, *Ages of Discord* 2016)**
HANDY's central result: any parameterization where elites consume at a higher per-capita rate than commoners produces collapse regardless of absolute resource abundance, because elite extraction depletes the pool faster than it regenerates while commoners lack the buffer to absorb shocks. Turchin's structural-demographic theory adds two further collapse channels: elite overproduction (elite families reproduce into a fixed number of high-status positions, generating surplus counter-elites who compete destructively) and fiscal crisis (elite resistance to taxation hollows out the state's buffering capacity, measured as a Political Stress Index that peaks 10–20 years before the collapse event). The current model captures the Gini signal but not the mechanism — all agents extract from the pool at the same rate formula regardless of wealth rank, so the HANDY result (high inequality causes collapse even at a healthy pool level) cannot emerge; introducing a consumption-rate multiplier scaled to relative resource rank would let Gini become a leading indicator of collapse from mechanism rather than from luck or theft alone.

### Behavioral feedback (research-grounded)

**Equilibrium-seeking agents with heterogeneous overcorrection (workaholics vs. monks)**
Today agents are reactive and stochastic — events fire *to* them; no agent pursues a goal. Give each a happiness/resource set-point and let them act to close the gap: below their happiness target they divert resources toward happiness-raising inputs (relationship formation, illness recovery, helping — `helpHappinessBoost` already exists), below their resource target they lean into gathering/work/theft at the cost of those inputs. Note happiness is a *computed getter*, not a stock — agents trade the *inputs* happiness is derived from, they don't barter two currencies. The point is **not** that the trade-offs are optimal: per-person miscalibration (set-point + a personal correction gain, both heritable via ARD 037) means the *same* drive produces opposite extremes — high-gain resource-seekers become **workaholics** (over-accumulate, low happiness), high-gain happiness-seekers become **monks** (renounce resources). That heterogeneity is the feature: it *generates* dispersion in resources (Gini, the primary collapse signal) and behavior rather than converging everyone on one set-point, and the corrective pullback is a candidate for the negative feedback the model currently lacks (crash recovery / population self-regulation — see the High-priority residual). This is the general parent of three narrower items below — hedonic adaptation, relative deprivation, and desistance are all special cases of "agents act on their own state." Big paradigm shift (utility/homeostatic agents), so do it as the smallest sharp lever first — one trade-off — and **sweep-validate** (`npm run sweep`) against the boom-bust baseline: does extinction-share fall *without* erasing outcome variety? Encodes non-obvious choices (set-point distribution, correction-gain spread, what's sacrificable for what) → ARD discussion before any code.

**Stress-weighted dissolution**
`BASE_BREAKUP_RATE` in ARD 025 is flat because there is no calibration anchor for how much of the empirical 3%/yr rate is baseline vs. economically-driven. Economic stress and financial disagreement are the strongest documented predictors of real-world partnership dissolution. A Gini- or resource-relative term would create a direct feedback loop: high inequality → more breakups → lower happiness → higher MisfortuneEvent mortality. Add once the flat-rate model produces enough sim data to observe whether dissolution rates actually correlate with Gini runs.

**Desistance: intent decay from stable conditions (Sampson & Laub)**
ARD 036 adds emboldening (permanent `stealingIntent` increase per undetected theft) but no counter-mechanism. Empirically, criminal careers peak and then decline as people age, form stable relationships, and gain employment. Intent decay tied to employment, partnership duration, or age would complete the arc and prevent emboldened persons from remaining at peak criminal propensity indefinitely.

**Hedonic adaptation on resources (Easterlin; Kahneman-Deaton)**
Happiness from resources should be log-shaped, not linear. Without diminishing returns, wealthy persons accumulate unbounded happiness — contradicts the empirical decoupling of wealth from life satisfaction.

**Relative deprivation in `happiness` (Luttmer; Wilkinson & Pickett)**
Happiness depends on resources relative to peers. A `(myResources - localMedian) / localMedian` term makes inequality directly costly to wellbeing rather than only correlated via Gini. Requires a proximity definition.

**Generalized trust as a per-person stat (Putnam)**
Victimization is currently recorded but has no behavioral consequence on the victim — antisocial behavior can't degrade social cohesion. A per-person trust score damaged by appearing in others' StealingRecord/KillingRecord and slowly restored by neutral interactions would close that loop and gate positive-sum events.

**Cultural norm erosion / cooperation propensity (Henrich 2015; Ostrom 1990; Boyd, Gintis, Bowles & Richerson, *PNAS* 2003; Gavrilets & Richerson, *PNAS* 2017)**
Ostrom's commons governance research shows cooperative extraction restraint is sustained only by active norm enforcement (graduated sanctions, participatory rule-making); when sanctioning capacity erodes — via inequality, elite defection from shared rules, or population disruption — overextraction accelerates independently of any individual's intent change. Henrich's collective brain thesis frames this as a cultural transmission failure: cooperative knowledge accumulates intergenerationally and collapses faster than it was built when transmission is disrupted. The current model has no feedback where rising inequality erodes willingness to restrain extraction; a cooperation-propensity parameter that decays as Gini rises (Putnam's trust–inequality correlation is empirically robust across countries and US states) and recovers as HelpEvent frequency rises would make the pool directly sensitive to social cohesion — adding the upstream mechanism that drives the commons toward the HANDY collapse trajectory before individual intents visibly change.

**Aggregate cultural morality / social norm level**
No event currently reads population-level state besides Gini — `KILL_GINI_SCALAR` raises kill *attempts* with inequality, the one aggregate feedback that exists. There's no scalar representing collective moral climate that damps `stealingIntent`/`killingIntent` and lifts `helpingIntent` (or the event probabilities directly) as a function of the population's own recent antisocial/prosocial rate, working opposite the individual-level pressures already modeled (situational steal/kill multipliers, theft emboldening). Distinct from "Cultural norm erosion / cooperation propensity" above, which targets a person's own extraction restraint on the commons pool — this would apply to the StealEvent/KillEvent/HelpEvent gates directly. The two could plausibly unify into one social-cohesion parameter feeding both channels; worth discussing together rather than as separate mechanisms. Needs a global-vs-local scope decision and calibration for how strongly it responds to and feeds back on Gini/crime rate. ARD-level (new aggregate state + non-obvious calibration); discuss with owner first.

### Social structure

**Proximity (Tobler; Christakis & Fowler contagion)**
Many of the mechanisms above are local, not global — relative deprivation, contagion, threshold cascades, mortality exposure. The model has no proximity. Three shapes, cheapest to costliest:

1. **Coarse neighborhood label** — assign each person to one of M groups at seed; same label = proximate. Unlocks in-group bias, local norm drift, local comparison.
2. **Social graph** — explicit edges (kin, relationships, work ties). Matches Dunbar / Christakis-Fowler. Memory O(N·k).
3. **2D spatial grid** — coordinates per person; pool can localize. Heaviest; changes many event signatures.

Single decision point because relative deprivation, threshold cascades, bereavement, and contagion all need *some* proximity to be implementable.

**Collective violence / civil violence threshold (Keeley 1996; Epstein, *PNAS* 2002; Turchin; Diamond 2005)**
Individual KillEvent models independent homicide; organized group violence requires a coordination threshold — agents with shared grievance (high Gini, low happiness) mobilize when local enforcement capacity falls below an activation point, producing punctuated nonlinear outbursts rather than a smooth mortality gradient. Epstein's 2002 PNAS civil violence ABM reproduces this pattern with grievance = f(hardship − perceived legitimacy) gated by local cop density; Diamond identifies coordinated inter-clan warfare accelerating terminal decline in Easter Island and Maya cases specifically after resource stress crossed a threshold. A cluster of high-killingIntent agents that can coordinate would be capable of destroying the commons pool in a single episode — qualitatively different from per-person mortality and able to flip a STABLE trajectory into COLLAPSE, which individual KillEvent cannot. Does not strictly require full proximity infrastructure; a coarse faction-label approach would suffice.

**Multi-tier execution modes**
Three execution tiers behind a common `SimulationEngine` interface: (1) current OOP loop — easy to step through and inspect; (2) CPU performance mode using `Float32Array` stride layout — same logic, flat data, faster iteration, still debuggable via index arithmetic; (3) WebGPU compute mode — agents packed into GPU storage buffers, logic ported to WGSL shaders, orders-of-magnitude throughput for population sizes the CPU loop can't sustain. The interface contract is same-seed → same outcomes; a parity check against the reference engine validates each new tier. Requires an ARD to fix the memory layout (stride, field alignment), the PRNG strategy (per-agent seeding on GPU vs. shared state on CPU), and how relation fields (`killed[]`, `hasChildren[]`) that can't fit in a flat buffer are handled. Value is unlocking population scales where emergence and tipping-point dynamics become statistically observable. **Prerequisite (`docs/research-population-scaling.md`, 2026-09-14): the commons constants are fixed absolute numbers, so raising the population without raising them measures scarcity, not scale** — 10× the founding population on the default pool yields only 1.9× the peak, against 15.6× when the pool scales with it. Run at 10,000 agents against a 10,000-unit pool this work would observe starvation at one resource unit per person. Decide how the commons scales with population before building the engine that needs it.

> **Revised 2026-09-14 — the premise was wrong about why the engine was slow, and four details of the entry need correcting.** Tick cost was quadratic in population for algorithmic reasons, not layout ones: four full-population scans per person per tick (`resourceGini` in `KillEvent`, plus `getRandomOther`, `kill` and `indexOfLiving` in `Simulation`). Removing them, with no layout change at all, gave **~19× on the default config (10,421 ms → 554 ms over 300 ticks × 4 seeds) and at every population size up to 10,000, bitwise-identical across 32 seeds × 300 ticks at 100 persons and 4 seeds × 100 ticks at 1,000** — see `docs/research-engine-performance.md`. So tier 2 is not the fix for current slowness and should be sequenced last; `scripts/parity-check.ts` already exists as the harness the tier contract needs. Four corrections to the design as written: (1) use **`Float64Array`, not `Float32Array`** — the headline metric is a Gini, a ratio of sums over the whole population, and f32's ~7 digits will drift it, while the memory saved at 100k agents is ~6 MB and buys nothing; (2) prefer **struct-of-arrays over the stride layout**, since a column per field is far easier to extend and the AoS advantage for this agent-major loop is noise next to the allocation wins; (3) the **same-seed → same-outcomes contract is unachievable for tier 3** — WGSL has no f64, so the GPU tier can only promise statistical equivalence across seeds, and only tier 2 should be held to bitwise parity; (4) the relation fields are **not actually a problem** — every unbounded structure (`killed`, `amountStolen`, `hasChildren`) is read only through its count or a single living-children filter, and `childOf` is capped at 2 by the `Person` constructor, so they collapse to `Int32Array` counters plus a child linked list (`firstChild` per parent, `nextSibling` per parent slot). One real constraint the entry misses: the model runs off a single shared LCG stream, so parity forbids the natural SoA move of running one event across a whole column. Field-major batching is legal only for RNG-free passes (`collectTax`, consumption, stat decay); anything that rolls stays person-major in shuffled order.

**Freeze the inequality signal at tick start (ARD-level; the last quadratic term in the engine)**
`KillEvent` reads `resourceGini` over the whole living population, so the value it multiplies into the attempt probability drifts *within* a tick as the people ahead in the iteration order gather, consume and steal. That is an iteration-order artifact in the model's core inequality→violence loop, and `LooperSingleton` shuffles the population precisely to keep iteration order out of the results — so this reintroduces the bias the shuffle exists to remove. Computing the Gini once at tick start and reading that fixed value for every person removes it, and is also arguably the better model: inequality is a societal signal an agent perceives, not something that updates between their neighbour's harvest and their own.
It is also where the remaining performance ceiling sits: after the 2026-09-14 optimisation pass the engine is linear in population except for this call, which is now bracketed so it fires for only ~3% of person-ticks but still scans the whole population when it does — at n=3200 that residual is 89% of runtime. Freezing it per tick is worth roughly another 9× at that size. The two exact alternatives both fail (a cache is stale immediately because everyone's resources change every tick; incremental maintenance sums in a different order and loses bitwise parity), so **bitwise-exact and linear-time are mutually exclusive here** — which is why this needs an ARD rather than another refactor. Expect outcomes to move: a probe shifted peak population from 722 to 467 at n=400, so the ARD must report a seed sweep, not a single run. Measurements and the rejected alternatives: `docs/research-engine-performance.md`.

## Might be droppable

Niche, dependent on heavier infrastructure, or substantially overlapping a higher-tier idea.

### Mechanics

**Disaster pod/proximity targeting**
ARD 012 selects disaster victims randomly from the full population. Real disasters affect physical clusters. Refinement of an existing mechanic; only meaningful with proximity built, and even then the random-victim model already produces clustered loss in aggregate.

**Previous suicide attempts increase future risk**
A prior attempt is the strongest empirical predictor of future suicide. Persons currently have no memory of past crises. Niche — suicide is already in MisfortuneEvent; the second-order amplification is unlikely to swing collapse/thrive verdicts.

### Behavioral feedback (research-grounded)

**Relative deprivation in steal probability (Blau & Blau; World Bank)**
Cross-national data shows a one-decile Gini rise associates with ~4% more property crime; the mechanism is relative deprivation, not absolute poverty. The StealEvent formula could include a `(victim.resources - thief.resources)` gap term so stealing scales with visible inequality rather than thief intent alone. This would create a direct feedback loop — high Gini → more theft → more Gini — that the current formula (intent × ageModifier) can't produce. Requires deciding whether gap-scaling belongs in the formula or in an intent-update mechanism like strain theory above; they are distinct intervention points.

**Tolerated theft equilibrium (Blurton Jones; MIT Artificial Life)**
Primate and multi-agent research shows theft naturally gives way to reciprocal exchange when resource variance is high and groups are small. Packets of intermediate resource size are most targeted; very rich and very poor targets are both avoided (high risk vs. low reward). If StealEvent runs long enough, a stable reciprocal-exchange norm could emerge without any explicit cooperation mechanic — worth measuring in simulation runs to see if it appears. Depends on StealEvent and relationship event both being live.

**Threshold heterogeneity for cascades (Granovetter)**
Per-person threshold for joining antisocial behavior based on observed prevalence. Captures riots / norm collapse tipping points. At ~100-person populations the dynamics may not be visible; arguably interesting only at larger scale.

**Bereavement / exposure-to-death effect**
Persons who experience nearby deaths shift toward lower risk-taking and stronger in-group preference. Connects the existing death stream to surviving behavior. Niche; depends on proximity. (TMT priming literature has replication issues, but the broader bereavement effect is well-supported.)

### Social structure

**Dunbar-bounded social cognition**
Cognitive cap on stable relationships (~150 in the original argument). Distinct from proximity (which others are near vs. how many a person can track). At the current ~100-person scale the cap never binds; relevant only if simulations grow well past 150.
